# Capacity, opportunity and attrition experiment protocol

Continue from cc80da73; preserve all existing caches, scores and raw datasets. No network collection or production mutation is required for this experiment.

Hypothesis: a population workload prior followed by a cohort workload-survival multiplier does not condition sufficiently on individual durability, debut/promotion history, observed role, or recent MLB opportunity. This is not assumed to be a literal duplicate medical penalty. Test direct one-year expected MLB workload, and a two-stage participation × conditional workload model. Both learn attrition once. MiLB debut innings enter only capacity features; actual talent rates remain MLB-only. Include a no-capacity ablation, a bounded pandemic-feature variant, and linear/direct-tree alternatives. No player-name feature, manual player gate, or projection overrides.

Fixed models before new scoring: standardized ridge(alpha100); gradient boosting squared-error mean workload (100 iterations, 15 leaves, minimum30 samples, learning rate.07,l2=10); and gradient-boosting participation probability times conditional mean workload with the same fixed settings. A bounded2020 variant uses min(actual*162/60, highest previously recorded same-role workload) for workload features only. Targets and talent remain actual MLB counts. Training excludes shortened2020 targets and2020 anchors in primary methods; COVID variants and raw-training diagnostics are separated. No outcomes later than forecast date enter any fitted model. DOB/stable identities are not prediction-date outcomes.

Chronological anchors:2016,2017,2018,2021,2022,2023,2024. Preserve exact1084 pitcher test identities/cases. Expand hitters using the same as-of protocol. ID%5==0 is the held-out test population; model selection uses ID%5==4 and training IDs%5 in1,2,3. Fit final models on non-test identities only, with completed training targets<=anchor. Select one method per role from development errors normalized by role exposure; test scores do not choose hyperparameters. Also report every fixed alternative and capacity ablation. Previously exposed historical test labels are explicitly not claimed to be pristine prospective validation.

Hitter cohorts are defined from pre-forecast evidence: durable=4 consecutive calendar seasons>=600PA, ordinary regular=latest>=400PA without durable status, aging>=33, repeated-workload-shortfall=at least2 recent calendar seasons<400PA after MLB debut (availability proxy, not injury diagnosis). Pitchers retain first/second MLB season, young<=26, established, SP/RP, swingman, and recent promotion groups. Pandemic seasons are never counted as a personal low-workload event without schedule context. Report PA/IP MAE, signed bias,RMSE and category effects. Include missing-next-season targets as zero; do not select only survivors.

Current sensitivity is first-year only: replace expected PA/IP exactly once, preserving frozen MLB rate and rate-aging inputs and years2–8. Do not extrapolate one-year evidence into new eight-year dynasty paths. Saves/holds remain baseline opportunity forecasts in this workload-only candidate; increased capacity does not create closer/setup opportunities. Two-way streams are evaluated separately; prospects without MLB keep original forecast paths and evidence ledger. Values/competitive fit are recomputed from changed first-year utility. Distribution uncertainty, role transitions and longer horizons are limitations, not certified by point MAE.

Release gates: past-only preprocessing and fitted training dates, complete/flagged workload provenance, frozen exactQA3 definition, aggregate and key subgroup error/bias checks, current integrity checks, no concealed dynasty inflation, and user review before any numerical deployment. A method that improves aggregates but harms durable/young/debut subgroups remains unready or explicitly restricted for further study, not silently adopted.
